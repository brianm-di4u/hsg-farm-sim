# Hot Springs & Garland County History Timeline (by era)

Era-by-era history of Hot Springs and Garland County from deep prehistory to October 2026, organized by the corpus's seven shared era ids. Each era has a narrative, daily life, economy/jobs, prices, transport, buildings standing, farming/timber and a dated-event table merged from all seven research notes. Main sources are the Encyclopedia of Arkansas (EOA), National Park Service (NPS) pages and brochures, Wikipedia and the Arkansas Democrat-Gazette (ADG); items marked "(via search summary)" or `(snippet)` came from search-engine summaries and are one level less verified.
Use it as the backbone for Valley of Vapors: Through the Ages, the Trivia Trail and event cards in Bathhouse Barons. Conflicts are kept side by side with `conflict:`.

Era ids: ancient (<1804) · frontier (1804–1874) · gilded-spa (1875–1920) · roaring (1921–1946) · midcentury (1947–1967) · revival (1968–1999) · modern (2000–2026)

Source abbreviations (all links inline): EOA = Encyclopedia of Arkansas; NPS = National Park Service (nps.gov/hosp and npshistory.com mirrors); ADG = Arkansas Democrat-Gazette; HMdb = Historical Marker Database.

## Era: ancient (<1804)

### Narrative
- The first inhabitants of the Garland County area arrived about 12,000 BC — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). The EOA park entry puts the first immigrants in Arkansas "by 9500 BC" and says there is no evidence of how, or whether, they used the springs — [EOA Hot Springs National Park](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- NPS says people have been in the park "for over 10,000 years". The park has 92 known archaeological sites "and counting", including prehistoric novaculite quarries, campsites and cemeteries; artifacts include novaculite projectile points and grinding stones (a sign of long-term occupation) — [NPS Archeology](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/archeology.htm).
- The park consults with the Absentee Shawnee Tribe, the Caddo Nation, the Osage Nation and the Quapaw Tribe; survey methods include LiDAR, ground-penetrating radar and test units — [NPS Archeology](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/archeology.htm).
- Caddo confederations controlled the area from about AD 800 into early historic times, until European diseases pushed the survivors south — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/). Native people occupied the area until about AD 1600; artifacts suggest a link to the historic Caddo — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- In the 14th–15th centuries AD, mounds were built over burned buildings along the Ouachita River (found by archaeologist Mark R. Harrington); at one riverside camp his workers dug about 17 feet down through occupation remains — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- Sparse late-1700s records suggest Native Americans visiting the springs were displaced individuals rather than intact tribes — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- Bathing: conflict: EOA's county entry says "no historic evidence" shows local Native residents bathed in the hot water — [EOA Garland County (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); an NPS-history publication says old documents show American Indians knew of and bathed in the springs in the late 1700s and early 1800s — [npshistory.com HOSP publications (via search summary)](https://www.npshistory.com/publications/hosp/index.htm). Reconciliation: evidence for late-1700s/1800s use, none for the deep past.
- **[LEGEND]** "Valley of the Vapors"/neutral ground: EOA says legends of the springs as neutral ground for rival tribes are later tourism-era embellishments — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Larry Foley (U. of Arkansas journalism chair) called the peace story "largely mythology" — [USA Today via PressReader, 2021-03-19 (via search summary)](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/usa-today-us-edition/20210319/282140704144680). The nickname comes from steam rising off the springs — [npshistory.com (via search summary)](https://www.npshistory.com/publications/hosp/index.htm).
- **[MYTH vs REALITY]** De Soto, 1541: the expedition crossed the Mississippi June 28, 1541 (June 18 Julian), spent about 11 months in Arkansas, and de Soto died May 31, 1542 — [EOA Route of the de Soto Expedition](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/route-of-the-de-soto-expedition-7679/). The 1939 Swanton commission route ran the expedition into the Ouachitas; its vice chair John R. Fordyce of Hot Springs "had a business interest in showing that the expedition discovered the location of Hot Springs"; EOA says it "undoubtedly introduced significant errors"; Charles Hudson's route is "generally considered to be the best approximation" — [EOA de Soto route](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/route-of-the-de-soto-expedition-7679/). Spanish explorers passed through the county in 1541 but there is "no firm proof" de Soto visited the springs — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). The 1920 NPS brochure said only he "may have" visited — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm). The expedition fought the Tula ("clearly not Caddo") in October 1541 near Fort Smith — [EOA de Soto route](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/route-of-the-de-soto-expedition-7679/). A life-size de Soto statue still stands in the Fordyce men's bath hall — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/).
- Colonial claims: Marquette and Jolliet claimed the area for France in 1673 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). France claimed Arkansas 1682–1762 and ceded Louisiana to Spain in 1762 — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); conflict: Wikipedia gives the 1763 Treaty of Paris (1762 secret Treaty of Fontainebleau vs 1763 public treaty). Spain returned it to France in 1800; French place names such as Fourche a Loupe point to 18th-century French presence — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). Louisiana Purchase agreed April 30, 1803 at about 3¢ an acre — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- **[inferred]** Honest game framing: "Caddo-ancestral homeland, later Quapaw-claimed hunting ground, steam valley visited by travelers"; neutral-ground truce and de Soto bathing shown only as in-world legends (e.g. told by a 1920s tour guide or bathhouse muralist).

### Daily life
- Long-term occupation shown by grinding stones and campsites; riverside camps along the Ouachita with deep occupation deposits — [NPS Archeology](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/archeology.htm); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- Wildlife recorded at the very end of this era (1804): swans, deer, raccoons, signs of buffalo — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/). Extirpated since settlement: plains bison, eastern elk, cougar and Gregory's (red) wolf — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- Landscape: oak/hickory on north slopes, shortleaf pine on south slopes — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).

### Economy and jobs
- Novaculite quarrying and trade, "called Arkansas's oldest industry" — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- Novaculite: hard, dense, white-to-gray-black microcrystalline quartz; Latin "razor stone"; conchoidal fracture; Ouachita formations about 250–900 ft thick. Grades: "Washita" (dull, porous, like unglazed porcelain) and "Arkansas" (very fine-grained, waxy, denser); top whetstone grade mined almost only in Garland and Hot Spring counties — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/). Also occurs in pink, red, tan, dark gray and black; found only in the Ouachitas (AR/OK) and southwest Texas; more than 120 prehistoric quarries recorded in Arkansas — [Arkansas Archeological Survey PDF](https://archeology.uark.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Arkansas-Novaculite.pdf).
- EOA lists Quapaw, Osage, Caddo, Tunica, Chickasaw and Natchez as novaculite miners (no dates or per-group evidence; treat as general); novaculite was traded over much of North America — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- A 1905 Shinn textbook describes "ancient" quarries in Hot Spring and Garland counties, some running about 4 miles; pits 15–40 ft deep, 10–30 ft wide, 100–300 ft long; flakes covered hundreds of square miles — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- French trappers used novaculite whetstones to sharpen skinning knives — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).

### Prices and money
- 1803 Louisiana Purchase: about 3¢ an acre — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). No other sourced prices.

### Transport
- Foot and river travel along the Ouachita (inferred from riverside camps); Europeans arrived by river (de Soto 1541; Dunbar–Hunter 1804 by boat up the Ouachita) — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/).

### Buildings standing in this era
- No permanent European buildings. Mounds over burned buildings along the Ouachita (1300s–1400s). Novaculite quarry pits. Health seekers' cabin and shacks existed by Dec. 1804 (see `frontier`) — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/). The 1920 NPS brochure says the earliest white settlement is believed to date to about 1800 — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).

### Farming, crops and timber
- No sourced crop data for this era. Forest of shortleaf pine, post oak, pine–oak–hickory; endemic maple-leaf oak (only four sites worldwide, all in the Ouachitas) — [Wikipedia: Ouachita Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Mountains).

## Era: frontier (1804–1874)

### Narrative
- **Dunbar–Hunter Expedition (1804–05).** One of four Jefferson expeditions into the Louisiana Purchase; Congress appropriated $3,000 — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/). Aug. 17, 1804 Dunbar wrote Jefferson about "the boiling springs"; left St. Catherine's Landing Oct. 16; Fort Miro (Monroe, La.) Nov. 6; entered Arkansas Nov. 15; Nov. 22 Hunter's pistol injured his thumb and two fingers; passed the Chutes rapids Dec. 3; camped at Calfait (Gulpha) Creek/Ellis Landing Dec. 7; Dunbar and Hunter walked to the springs Dec. 8 — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/). conflict: EOA park entry gives a stay of Dec. 9, 1804–Jan. 8, 1805 — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- Party of 19: 13 soldiers, Hunter's teenage son, two enslaved men belonging to Dunbar, and a servant. Hunter's Pittsburgh-built "Chinese-style vessel" drew too much water, so Dunbar got a flatboat at Fort Miro and hired guide Samuel Blazier — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/).
- At the springs: an empty cabin and several visitor-built shacks; four principal and two lesser springs, water averaging 148–150°F; microorganisms living in the hot water (possibly the first such report); a "cabbage raddish of the Washita"; swans, deer, raccoons and buffalo sign; water source undetermined. They left Jan. 8, 1805, reached Natchez Jan. 27, 1805; results published 1806; Dunbar's journals reached Jefferson more than a year before Lewis and Clark returned. Dunbar (b. 1749, Elgin, Scotland) d. 1810; Hunter (Philadelphia chemist) d. Feb. 23, 1823 — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/). On the way home in 1805 they met possibly Quapaw Indians led by Jean LeFevre — same source. Dunbar–Hunter found the whetstone rock in 1804; its deposits became the main American whetstone source until artificial whetstones in the 1970s — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/).
- **First settlers.** 1807: Jean Pierre Emanuel (Manuel) Prudhomme built the first real settlement and stayed two years; trappers Isaac Cates and John Percival joined him, and Percival built cabins to rent to visitors — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm) (spells "Perciful" and "Isaac Gates"). conflict: EOA park entry names Revolutionary War veteran John Percifull "probably the first permanent settler" (1807–1809), offering room and board — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/); EOA city entry says John Perciful in 1809 is "likely the first permanent white settler" — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- Aug. 24, 1818: the Quapaw ceded the land around the hot springs to the U.S. — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/). Whetstone mining began as early as 1818 in the Hot Springs region — [Arkansas Archeological Survey PDF](https://archeology.uark.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ArkansasNovaculite-rev_acc.pdf).
- Arkansas Territory 1819 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). 1820 territorial petition to reserve the springs; nothing came of it — [NPS History & Culture](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm); [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- 1825 first structure considered a hotel — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). 1828 Ludovicus Belding and family from Massachusetts built a small hotel within months — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); Beldings at the springs no later than 1828 — [EOA Rector v. US](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/).
- **April 20, 1832 reservation.** President Andrew Jackson signed the act reserving the springs and surroundings (2,529.1 acres) and barring settlement; the territorial government had asked for hospital funds in 1832 — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/). The 1920 brochure calls it the springs plus four surrounding sections, "the country's first national park reservation" — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm). See hot-springs-national-park.md.
- Arkansas statehood 1836 — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). Reservation placed under the new Interior Department, 1849 — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- **Land claims.** Hale, Belding, Percival and Rector fought ownership in court for almost 25 years — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/) (the overall fight lasted about 45 years from 1832 to 1877). Details in hot-springs-national-park.md.
- Town incorporated Jan. 10, 1851 with two rows of hotels and bathhouses — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); conflict: Wikipedia history section gives April 5, 1876 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Civil War.** After Pea Ridge (March 1862) Gov. Henry M. Rector moved staff and records to Hot Springs, state capital May 6–July 14, 1862 — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). NPS calls Rector a bathhouse owner and politician — [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm). Almost the whole population fled to Texas and Louisiana; Union troops never occupied the town, but "renegade bands" threatened the county; the 1864 skirmish at Farr's Mill was the only notable fight — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). Two minor skirmishes Feb. 4, 1864 — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). After Union troops took Little Rock in September 1863, guerrillas pillaged and burned the near-empty town — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). Soldiers from both armies passed through — [NPS Stories](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/stories.htm). Earlier Hale bathhouses were likely destroyed by raiders in the Civil War — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Postwar.** 1870 population about 1,200 (census 1,276); by 1873 six bathhouses and 24 hotels and boardinghouses — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). William G. Maurice (1859–1927) of St. Louis first came in 1870 — [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm). Mountain Valley Spring Co. began continuous bottling in 1871 — [EOA Central Avenue Historic District](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/); first sold as "Lockett's Spring Water" by pharmacist Peter E. Greene and brother John; first distributorship at 195 Central Ave. — [EoA: Mountain Valley Spring Water](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/mountain-valley-spring-water-2148/).
- Garland County created April 5, 1873 from parts of Hot Spring, Montgomery and Saline counties, Hot Springs as seat, named for Augustus H. Garland (governor 1874, U.S. senator 1876–85, U.S. attorney general 1885) — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). Before that, Rockport (near Malvern) was the county seat — [EOA](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Garland-County-770).
- Horsecars ran 1874–1887 under the Hot Springs Railroad Co. — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs Intracity Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Intracity_Transit). 1874: Reynolds announces Malvern–Hot Springs narrow-gauge railroad — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **[inferred]** Game framing: a 1807–1860 "frontier spa" homestead and inn sim (log cabins, rental huts, small hotel, hunting, novaculite trade) where a federal settlement ban leaves everyone a squatter with a disputed title. Slavery was part of the antebellum resort economy and must be depicted carefully, not omitted.

### Daily life
- Health seekers lived in cabins/shacks near the springs (1804 onward); Percival rented cabins; in the 1830s log cabins and a store were built for visitors — [NPS History & Culture](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm).
- 1830 census: 458 residents in Hot Spring County, 84 in Hot Springs Township — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- 1860: Hot Springs 201 residents; Hot Spring County had 613 enslaved people out of 5,635 residents; many worked in hotels, and one hotel advertised baths for enslaved people, who were then returned to their owners — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- Wartime: town emptied; refugees in Texas/Louisiana; guerrilla burning (1863) left few buildings — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).

### Economy and jobs
- Renting cabins to health seekers (Percival), hotel keeping (Belding, 1828), trapping (Cates, Percival), enslaved hotel labor — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- Novaculite: settlers sharpened axes and saws with it and traded it for frontier goods — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- Water bottling from 1871 (Mountain Valley) — [EoA: Mountain Valley](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/mountain-valley-spring-water-2148/).
- Bath attendants first mentioned in records 1875 (see `gilded-spa`).

### Transport
- River boat, wagon, horseback, then stagecoach (pre-1876) — [NPS Getting to the American Spa](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/kidsyouth/getting-to-hot-springs-national-park.htm). The 21-mile stage from Malvern took most of a day and risked breakdowns, floods and robbers — [EOA Hot Springs Railroad](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157). Horsecars from 1874 — [Wikipedia: Intracity Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Intracity_Transit).

### Buildings standing in this era
- 1804: one empty cabin + several shacks at the springs. 1807: Prudhomme settlement; Percival rental cabins. 1825: first "hotel." 1828: Belding small hotel. 1830s: log cabins and a store. 1851: two rows of hotels and bathhouses. 1862–63: town largely burned. 1873: six bathhouses, 24 hotels/boardinghouses. 1880s Victorian Superior and earlier Hale houses (Hale "originally built in 1883" per one source — see `gilded-spa`). Few substantial buildings went up before the 1876 title ruling — [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/).
- The main street was Valley Street (renamed Central Avenue in the late 1870s) — [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/).
- **[inferred]** Visual: unpainted pine boards and log browns, whitewash, mud-brown street; buildings in one or two rows hugging the creek in the narrow valley (unsourced look; visual_style.md Gap).

### Farming, crops and timber
- Rocky soil in the northern county prevented large-scale farming; families grew wheat and raised cattle on small farms, eating the produce or selling it in Hot Springs; the Watson and then Ross families ran a water-powered corn mill on the South Fork of the Saline River; charcoal works, stave mills and shingle mills operated there; still heavily timbered — [EOA Whittington (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/whittington-garland-county-10724/) (dates for the mill not given; applies across `frontier`–`gilded-spa`).
- 1830 Preemption Act covered those farming in 1829 (relevant to the Belding claim) — [EOA Rector](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/).
- **[inferred]** Defensible crops/livestock: corn (milled locally), wheat, beef cattle, market gardens for hotels, staves/shingles/charcoal; novaculite whetstones as side job. Cotton minor or absent — no source tied it to Garland County.

## Era: gilded-spa (1875–1920)

### Narrative
- From about 1,276 people (1870) to a nationally marketed spa of about 14,000–16,000 (1910 census 14,434; NPS 1920 brochure ~16,000). Key steps: Diamond Jo Reynolds's narrow-gauge railroad (built 1875–76), the first Arlington Hotel (1875), the Government Free Bathhouse (1878), the creek arch (1882–84), the first combined Army-Navy hospital (1887), spring training (from 1886), Oaklawn (1904/1905) and brick-and-stucco Bathhouse Row (1892–1923). Fires repeatedly destroyed downtown (1878, 1905, 1913) and drove the move to brick — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Railroad.** Joseph "Diamond Jo" Reynolds (1819–1891), a Chicago tanner, grain trader and Mississippi steamboat owner, marked shipments "Jo" inside a diamond (the Diamond Jo Line); he went to Malvern to treat rheumatism — [EOA Hot Springs Railroad](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157). **[LEGEND]** On his stagecoach trip the coach broke down and riders walked to a farm for the night, after which he vowed to build a railroad; NPS dates the trip to 1878, after its own stated 1875 completion — [NPS Getting to the American Spa](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/kidsyouth/getting-to-hot-springs-national-park.htm). Construction began 1875 (April 1875 per Wikipedia); the line opened 1876 with last spikes Jan. 25, 1876 — [EOA HS Railroad](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157); [Wikipedia Hot Springs Railroad (via search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Railroad); [Malvern Daily Record via PressReader (via search summary)](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/malvern-daily-record/20210205/281590948250213). conflict: NPS says 1875 — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/kidsyouth/getting-to-hot-springs-national-park.htm); Wikipedia (city) says completed 1875 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). 3 ft gauge; trip cut from all day to one hour; rosewood and mahogany interiors, silk curtains, velvet drapes, leather seats; the only line into town for 25 years; converted to standard gauge Oct. 16, 1889 in about three hours, letting private Pullman cars run to town — [EOA HS Railroad](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157); [Wikipedia HSRR (via search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Railroad).
- 1877: the St. Louis, Iron Mountain & Southern published "The Hot Springs of Arkansas, America's Baden-Baden", the first booklet advertising the town — [NPS Getting to the American Spa](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/kidsyouth/getting-to-hot-springs-national-park.htm). "Great American Spa" was a nickname from the bathing industry — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm).
- 1899 attempt at a second line failed; Fordyce bought it; the Little Rock & Hot Springs Western later linked Memphis, Little Rock, Hot Springs and Texas — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/kidsyouth/getting-to-hot-springs-national-park.htm). The Choctaw, Oklahoma & Gulf took the Hot Springs Railroad in 1901; Rock Island acquired the CO&G in 1902 — [EOA HS Railroad](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157). By 1920: Rock Island, Iron Mountain (Missouri Pacific) and Memphis, Dallas & Gulf, with daily through sleepers from Chicago and Memphis — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- **Title and commission.** U.S. Supreme Court (October 1875 term) rejected three appealed claims; Wikipedia gives decision date April 24, 1876; EOA county dates final settlement 1877 — [EOA Rector v. US](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). 1877 act set boundaries, sold excess lots, taxed thermal water, appointed a superintendent, compensated improvements — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/). Townsite was 196 blocks and about 50 miles of streets; reservation 264.93 acres — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). Superintendent Benjamin Franklin Kelley (1807–1891) arrived October 1877 — [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm); his 1880s engineering made the Victorian bathhouses possible — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). June 1880 Congress added remaining mountain lands — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). Samuel W. Fordyce (1840–1919), railroad magnate and key promoter — [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm) — moved to Hot Springs in 1876 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Free Bathhouse and Ral City.** Government Free Bathhouse authorized Dec. 16, 1878 — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm); built over the "mud hole" spring for the poor — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); rebuilt in brick 1891, segregated plan 1898 — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm). Ral Spring, midway up Hot Springs Mountain at the foot of a tufa outcrop, probably the most famous 19th-century source; "Ral" likely from "neuralgia", a euphemism for syphilis symptoms; free access drew hundreds of poor health seekers to "Ral City"; by 1901 well drilling had sharply cut its flow — [NPS Ral Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/ral-spring.htm). A photo of "Ral City" dates from 1878 — [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm).
- **Bathhouse Row.** 1880–1888 larger Victorian bathhouses (last in 1888); 1882–1883 Hot Springs Creek enclosed in an underground arch (conflict: 1882–84 in some NPS summaries; designed for a 10-year flood); 1890s Army Capt. Robert R. Stevens oversaw landscaping; by 1901 all springs walled and covered; 1912–1923 brick and stucco replaced wood — [NPS History & Culture](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm); [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/); [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm); [NPS Hot Springs Creek (via search summary)](https://www.nps.gov/places/hot-springs-creek.htm). Surviving row: Hale 1892–93, Maurice 1911–12, Buckstaff 1912, Fordyce 1914–15, Superior 1916, then Quapaw/Ozark 1922 and Lamar 1923 — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/). Full building data in bathhouse-row-and-springs.md.
- **Hospital.** 1882, at a dinner on the second floor of the Palace Bathhouse, ex-Confederate surgeon Dr. A.S. Garnett hosted Sen. John A. Logan, a former Union general, who promised funding; late June 1882 Congress approved $100,000 for a 30-bed joint hospital, the first in U.S. history; Pres. Chester A. Arthur signed; opened January 1887 under the secretary of war — [EOA Army-Navy Hospital](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236). conflict: Wikipedia says 100 beds — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Fires.** 1878 (March 5): eight-hour fire, over 1,000 homeless — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); about 150 buildings, about $300,000 losses — [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/); conflict: marker says "almost 100 buildings, including the major portion of the business district" — [HMdb marker (via search summary)](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=211170); NPS: most of south and central downtown — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). First county courthouse (converted house, 1882) burned 1888 — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). 1905 (Feb. 25, ~3:00 a.m., Grand Central Hotel at Chapel & Crown, cause never found): water pressure failed ~5 a.m.; bucket brigades in pajamas; out ~9:30 a.m.; up to 25 died; more than 100 acres and up to 400 structures, about $2 million, incl. Grand Central and Moody hotels, the synagogue and the courthouse with its records; new codes required fire-resistant materials — [EOA Fire of 1905](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Hot-Springs-Fire-of-1905-12570); conflict: ADG gives 2 dead and about 500 homeless — [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/). 1913 (Friday Sept. 5, just before 2:15 p.m.): started in Ulma Madlock's Church Street home when ironing ignited a curtain (ADG: a charcoal stove heating irons at a Black-owned home laundry); City treasurer George Housley's men dynamited the People's Laundry at 356 Malvern Ave. without slowing it; Fire Chief Henry Higgins' hoses melted; the power plant burned (streetcars pulled by horses and mules); Little Rock's engine arrived 8 p.m. by train with hoses that did not fit; a thunderstorm put out the last of it. Losses $10 million; area conflict: 20 acres (EOA fire entry), ~50 blocks (ADG, NPS, EoA Crystal), 60 blocks (EOA county, Wikipedia); more than 2,500 homeless; at least 700 homes. Lost: Park Hotel ($500,000), Iron Mountain depot, Ozark Sanitarium, high school, Central Methodist, Crystal Bathhouse; courthouse gutted. Afterward wood-shingle roofs outlawed and the fire department doubled; Gov. George W. Hays arrived about midnight by special train; Pathé filmed it but the newsreels are lost — [EOA Fire of 1913](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Hot-Springs-Fire-of-1913-14306); [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/); [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [EoA: Crystal Bathhouse](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/crystal-bathhouse-4253/). The Peter Joplin Commercial Block (1905) was the only building in its area to survive — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); the Rock Island station was reportedly saved with Mountain Valley water and the Central Avenue bathhouses by a late wind shift — [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/).
- **Gambling and law.** Flynn–Doran feud (1882 wrecking of Jim K. Lane's clubs; Feb. 9, 1884 shootout), March 16, 1899 police vs sheriff gunfight (three officers killed) — see gangster-era-and-gambling.md. 1884: second superintendent Samuel Hamblen nearly shot on duty ("The Almost Shoot Out of 1884") — [NPS Stories](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/stories.htm). 1895 smallpox outbreak and a blizzard set back growth — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- **Science.** 1901: 44 springs analyzed; 1904 Bertram Boltwood found the waters radioactive (radium emanation); 1920: 46 hot springs (102–147°F, mostly 135–145°F) flowing 851,308 gallons/day — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- **Tourism scale.** In 1904 about 120,000 people visited at least one national park, and about 84% of them visited Hot Springs — [EOA Fire of 1905](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Hot-Springs-Fire-of-1905-12570).
- **Baseball and racing.** 1886 Chicago White Stockings (Cubs) first MLB team to train here (Cap Anson and A.G. Spalding, reportedly to "boil out" drinking) at the Hot Springs Baseball Grounds; Whittington Park 1894; Majestic Park 1908 (Wikipedia) or 1909 (Baseball Trail); Fogel Field 1912; March 17, 1918 Babe Ruth's estimated 573-ft homer into the Alligator Farm; 1912 Honus Wagner donated basketball uniforms to Hot Springs High (black and gold) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [Hot Springs Baseball Trail (via search summary)](https://hotspringsbaseballtrail.com/untold-stories/baseballs-golden-days-in-hot-springs/). Details in baseball-spring-training.md. Racing: Sportsman's Park early 1890s; anti-gambling laws repealed 1903; Essex Park 1904; Oaklawn Jockey Club formed 1904; Oaklawn opened Feb. 15, 1905 (~$500,000, 1,500 seats, glass-enclosed grandstand, steam heat) — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/); conflict: Wikipedia says opened Feb. 24, 1904 with 3,000+ and a city holiday — [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort). Feb. 27, 1907 anti-betting law closed it about a decade; State Fair in its infield 1906–1914 (Theodore Roosevelt 1910); Cella family owned by 1914; racing resumed March 11, 1916 for 30 days; Essex Park burned 1917 the day after reopening; ruled illegal again 1919 — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/). Details in oaklawn-and-racing.md.
- **Attractions.** Happy Hollow joke-photo studio (1888–1940s), Thomas Cockburn's Ostrich Farm (1900–1953), Arkansas Alligator Farm (1902) — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Whittington Park amusement area (early 1900s): ostrich farm, alligator farm, band pavilion, merry-go-rounds, roller coasters, vaudeville — [EoA: Whittington Park HD (snippet)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Whittington-Park-Historic-District-8545). See other-attractions.md.
- **Religion and institutions.** April 2–12, 1914 Pentecostal leaders formed the Assemblies of God — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). Levi Hospital founded 1914, still operates; Hot Springs Normal and Industrial Institute (Mebane Academy) for Black students, 1890s–1920s — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Hot Springs Special School District 1881; St. Mary's Academy 1882 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). Hot Springs High founded 1887; 1914 building (Sanguinet & Staats, Late Gothic Revival); Langston High for African American students from 1913 — [Wikipedia: HSHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_High_School_(Arkansas)).
- Ouachita National Forest established Dec. 18, 1907 as the Arkansas National Forest (renamed April 29, 1926), HQ in Hot Springs — [Wikipedia: Ouachita NF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_National_Forest).
- **[inferred]** "Brick vs. wood" upgrade mechanic: wooden buildings carry fire risk; after 1905 and 1913 the town forces rebuilding in fire-resistant brick and bans wood-shingle roofs. "America's Baden-Baden" (1877) is the right in-game marketing artifact.

### Daily life
- 1880s: Black patrons could buy tickets at the Ozark, Independent and possibly Rammelsberg bathhouses, but not during the 10 a.m.–noon hours doctors considered best — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- 1920 bather: a "course" of 21 baths prescribed by a federally registered physician; tipped an attendant within the 20¢ cap; stayed in a $7/week boarding house — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm). Free bathers swore a pauper's oath (false oath: fine up to $25, 30 days, or both); children admitted only as patients; 87,040 free baths in 1919 — same source.
- Visitors on the reservation mountain roads could drive autos 7 a.m.–6:30 p.m. (1920); more than 11 miles of roads and paths on 911.63 acres — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- The Majestic shuttled guests to the bathhouses every five minutes — [Abandoned Arkansas (via search summary)](https://abandonedar.com/the-majestic-hotel/) (era of the practice not stated).
- In 1899 the City Council barred "drummers" (fraudulent bath solicitors), but prosecution took about 30 more years; mercury rubs for syphilis; a clinic above the Free Bathhouse began Salvarsan treatments in 1920 — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- Ostrich races on a quarter-mile track, ostrich-pulled carts, children's rides; birds raced at State Fairs at Oaklawn (1906–1914); Central Avenue ostrich-products store 1911 — [EoA: Ostrich Farm (snippet)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/ostrich-farm-in-hot-springs-4429/). Happy Hollow comic photo backdrops (old bathtub, a bar); McLeod sold it in 1908 to Dave Anselberg — [EoA: Happy Hollow (snippet)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/happy-hollow-1206/).

### Economy and jobs
- Bath attendants (mentioned from 1875; mostly African American until the 1980s), masseurs (massage added 1890s), "mercury rubbers," porters and maids, licensed bath physicians, hotel staff, railroad crews, bookmakers and casino staff, spring-training ballplayers — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [NPS 1920](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- 1910: medical director Maj. Harry Hallock hired; classes and written tests for attendants — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- Black-owned bathing: William G. Maurice bought the Independent Bathhouse, which A.C. Page (African American) had run as a Black bathhouse for less than a year in 1890, and reopened it for whites only; Crystal Bathhouse (1904, 415 Malvern Ave., designed by John McCaslin) first built exclusively for African Americans; lease to Knights of Pythias 1908; burned 1913; Pythian Bathhouse and Sanitarium (W.T. Bailey of Tuskegee) opened Dec. 27, 1914 with half-price baths for Pythian insurance members — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- Illegal "drumming" (touts soliciting patients on trains) banned — [NPS 1920](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- Gator farming (hides, live sales to zoos) from 1902 (H. L. Campbell, gators from the Florida Everglades) — [EOA](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-alligator-farm-and-petting-zoo-20827/). Water bottling (Mountain Valley bought by W. N. Benton of St. Louis in 1879) — [EoA](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/mountain-valley-spring-water-2148/).
- Late-1800s county boom towns Bear, Blakely and White Sulphur Springs tied to timber or tourism; Bear started on unfounded rumors of precious metals — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- Utilities: Hot Springs Water Company formed 1882 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).

### Transport
- Stage from Malvern (21 miles, most of a day) until 1876; narrow-gauge rail 1876 (one hour); standard gauge with Pullmans 1889; three railroads by 1920 — [EOA HS Railroad](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157); [NPS 1920](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- Streetcars: horsecars 1874–1887 (Hot Springs Railroad Co.); conflict: electric cars replaced mule cars by 1893 (city marker) or 1895 (Wikipedia); Hot Springs Street Railway Co. 1910–1966; a marker calls it Arkansas's oldest continuously operating streetcar system, starting 1875 with two cars — [Wikipedia: Intracity Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Intracity_Transit); [Chicago Railfan](https://chicagorailfan.com/aatar2.html); [HMdb marker](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=211154). Electric streetcars running by 1913 (horse- and mule-pulled after the power plant burned) — [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/).
- Autos on reservation mountain roads 7 a.m.–6:30 p.m. (1920) — [NPS 1920](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).

## Era: roaring (1921–1946)

### Narrative
- NPS created Aug. 25, 1916; the reservation became the 18th national park on March 4, 1921 — [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). 1921: U.S. Public Health Service opened the nation's first federally run venereal disease clinic here — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- 1921–22 Free Bathhouse rebuilt with four separate sections (Black women, white women, Black men, white men) at Reserve and Spring streets — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). 1922 Quapaw and Ozark; 1923 Lamar completed the Row — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/). 1933 central thermal water collection and distribution system; Grand Promenade grading began. 1936 administration building (faces the street because its predecessor kept being mistaken for a bathhouse). 1937 Imperial Bathhouse razed — [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). Maurice added a 1931 therapeutic pool inspired by FDR's Warm Springs — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/).
- March 12, 1927: ranger James A. Cary became the first NPS ranger killed on duty — [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm).
- **Arlington.** Second Arlington burned April 5, 1923 (one fireman died, $1.6 million loss); detective William Pinkerton smoked a cigar on the veranda and lost all his belongings; third Arlington begun Oct. 25, 1923, off federal land, completed Nov. 28, 1924 in 11 months and 3 days — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/); architect George Mann — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/); Mann & Stern, Spanish Colonial Revival, 484 rooms, twin towers, opened by Dec. 31, 1924 (conflict: one mirror gives 1925) — [Wikipedia: Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_Hotel_(Hot_Springs_National_Park)). Amenities: Arthur Murray dance studio, roof gardens, a bathhouse with private elevator, gas station, 10-piece Meyer-Davis orchestra, the Fountain Room — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- 1928: Sen. Joe T. Robinson accepted the Democratic VP nomination on the Arlington's front steps — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/). KTHS ("Kum/Kome To Hot Springs" **[DISPUTED]**) built 1924 on the Arlington's upper floor; Class B license Dec. 19, 1924; tests Dec. 11; first regular broadcast Dec. 20, 1924, 8:30 p.m., from the ballroom; mostly live big-band music; Campbell Arnoux director to 1934; Chamber of Commerce took over May 27, 1928; launched Lum and Abner — [EOA KTHS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/kths-radio-station-6423/); [ADG 2008](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2008/mar/09/remembering-arkansas-hot-springs-kths-was-20080309/). conflict: the Arlington history page dates KTHS to 1928.
- **May 1923 flood** swept the Central Avenue business district, tossing cars and blowing manholes into the air — [1923 wire report (Nebraska newspapers)](https://nebnewspapers.unl.edu/lccn/sn84024326/1923-05-17/ed-1/seq-5.pdf); Superior damaged, not severely — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/).
- **Downtown skyline.** Wade Building 1927; Medical Arts Building, 16-story Art Deco by Almand & Stuck, begun Dec. 1, 1929 on the old Rector Bath House site, $375,000 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/); finished 1930, Arkansas's tallest until the Tower Building in Little Rock (1960) — [EoA Medical Arts](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/medical-arts-building-6047/). Marquette and Park hotels opened 1930; the Park seven stories by Thompson, Sanders & Ginocchio — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/). Manning family owned the Majestic 1929–2006 — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/); Majestic Hotel "1902, expanded 1926" per trail marker — [Trail site](https://hotspringsbaseballtrail.com/see-the-trail/).
- **Black Hot Springs.** Woodmen of Union Building (designed 1922 by W.T. Bailey, built 1923–24), 501 Malvern Ave.: hospital and nurse school, bath hotel, bank, theater, print shop; hosted Count Basie, Peg Leg Bates, Joe Louis; closed 1935 — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [Wikipedia Woodmen of Union Building (via search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodmen_of_Union_Building). 100-bed hospital, 75-room bath hotel, 2,500-seat auditorium — [Preserve Arkansas (via search summary)](https://preservearkansas.org/woodmen-of-union-building-national-baptist-hotel/); conflict: NPS gives a 2,000-seat theater plus 600-capacity auditorium. James Truman (Arlington, 1922) and Estella Hughey (Majestic, 1926) among the first Black masseurs/masseuses — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm). Pythian enlarged 1923 — [LOC HABS (via search summary)](https://www.loc.gov/item/ar0041).
- **Open town.** Mayor Leo P. McLaughlin (1927–1947) ran an "open town"; casinos paid regular "fines" that worked as license fees; Owney Madden settled 1935 (ADG: 1934); Capone, Luciano, Costello and Lansky visited — see gangster-era-and-gambling.md. Peak gambling 1927–1947 with at least 10 major casinos; 1937 an ex-sheriff who tried to enforce anti-gambling laws was murdered, no one charged — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Lakes and dams.** Remmel Dam (Lake Catherine) 1924 — [Wikipedia: Lake Catherine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Catherine_(Arkansas)). 1929 Arkansas Power & Light (Harvey Couch) began Carpenter Dam, finished two years later, creating Lake Hamilton — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); completed 1931, lake formed 1932 — [Wikipedia: Lake Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Hamilton_(Arkansas)). Lake Catherine State Park 1935 (CCC) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [Wikipedia: LCSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Catherine_State_Park). Lake Winona built July 1936–May 1938 by Little Rock Municipal Water Works — [EOA Lake Winona](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/lake-winona-6572/).
- **Oaklawn.** 1929 Osro Cobb's tie-breaking vote on racing — [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort); March 1, 1934 reopened without legislative consent before ~8,000; Gov. J.M. Futrell signed pari-mutuel bill 1935; first Arkansas Derby 1936 ($5,000) — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/). Charles Cella died Oct. 29, 1940; son John G. Cella took over — [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort). No Arkansas Derby in 1945 (WWII) — [KentuckyDerby.com (snippet)](https://kentuckyderby.com/?p=21445).
- **Baseball.** Homestead Grays at Fogel Field 1930–31, Pittsburgh Crawfords 1932–35; Whittington renamed Ban Johnson Park 1935 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [Baseball-Reference Bullpen (via search summary)](https://www.baseball-reference.com/bullpen/Ban_Johnson_Park). Sam Guinn Field (1933, 497 Crescent Ave.); Ray Doan school 1933–38; George Barr Umpire School 1935–47; Hornsby Baseball College 1939–47 (conflict: until 1952); Bathers in Cotton States League 1938–41 (title 1941) — [Wikipedia: Ban Johnson Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Johnson_Park); [Wikipedia: Bathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Bathers).
- **World War II.** New Army-Navy hospital completed 1933: seven stories, fireproof brick and steel, ~$1.5 million — [EOA Army-Navy Hospital](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236); conflict: Wikipedia six stories, and says $1.5M after WWI for a 500-patient hospital; Joe DiMaggio was a patient — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). 1887–WWII: more than 100,000 patients; in WWII the Army's leading arthritis center and largest U.S. adult-polio center; government bought the Eastman Hotel and connected it by ramp, roughly tripling beds; Arlington and Majestic housed overflow — [EOA ANH](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236). Military took the Eastman 1942 (demolished 1946); August 1944 the Army took most hotels for a redistribution center, closed December 1945 after processing more than 32,000 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). conflict: by Labor Day 1944 five hotels commandeered, ~3,000 servicemen a day "until the end of 1946"; at the Arlington the Fountain Room became the officers' mess, main dining room a mess hall, ballroom a movie theater — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/). Majestic an Army redistribution center about 18 months from 1944 — [Abandoned Arkansas (via search summary)](https://abandonedar.com/the-majestic-hotel/). Reynolds Metals opened in the county in 1942 — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); Bill Seiz secured an aluminum plant between Hot Springs and Malvern plus a shoe factory — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). 1941 Mitchell v. United States arose from Rep. Arthur Mitchell (African American congressman) being removed from a first-class rail car en route to Hot Springs — same.
- **1946.** Bathing peaked in 1946 with more than 1 million baths — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/); [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/); conflict: Wikipedia gives 649,270 baths on the Row as the 1946 record — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). Memorial Field dedicated 1946 as McLaughlin Field — [City of Hot Springs: Airport History](https://www.hotspringsar.gov/476/Airport-History). November 1946 GI ticket swept Garland County — [EOA GI Revolt](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/GI-Revolt-4157).
- **[inferred]** "Two economies" mechanic (legal: regulated baths, hotels, racing; underground: casinos, wire rooms, bookies, slot routes paying "fines"); a WWII chapter swaps tourists for soldiers (hotels as barracks, ballrooms as theaters, farm sells to the hospital mess).

### Daily life
- McLaughlin rode down Central Avenue daily in a sulky pulled by horses "Scotch and Soda," in riding clothes with a red carnation; gamblers paid fines; city employees passed out pink-slip ballots — [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/).
- 1936 bath rules: physicians had to pass the federal board and register with the superintendent; exam fees $5–$10; attendant fees still capped at 20¢/bath or $4/21-bath course — [NPS 1936 brochure (via search summary)](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/brochures/1936/hosp/sec3.htm).
- Capone often stayed at the Arlington in the 1930s, renting the fourth floor with Room 443 as his suite; a clerk once insisted he wear a tie to dinner — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/) (Room 443 vs 442 **[DISPUTED]**, see gangster-era-and-gambling.md).
- Interstate Orphanage (now Ouachita Children's Center) built 1928 with volunteer labor — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- McClard's: Alex and Gladys McClard ran the Westside Tourist Court in the 1920s; by 1928 goat barbecue (sauce recipe traded for an unpaid $10 two-month bill); moved 1942 to the whitewashed stucco building at 505 Albert Pike — [EoA: McClard's](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/mcclards-bar-b-q-4230/).
- Record temperatures: −11°F (Feb. 1930) and 115°F (1936; again 1986) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Wartime: hotels as barracks and messes; polio and arthritis patients; ~3,000 servicemen a day at peak — [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).

### Economy and jobs
- Bath economy (attendants, masseurs, physicians), hotel staff, casino staff, bookmakers, racing wire, racing (jockeys, trainers), radio musicians/announcers (from 1924), baseball school instructors — [NPS AfAm](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [EOA KTHS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/kths-radio-station-6423/).
- Novaculite mining added to the county economy in the 1920s–30s; Dierks Forests built a major timber industry — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- 1942 onward: aluminum (Reynolds) and a shoe factory — [EOA GC](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Army-Navy Hospital among the county's top five employers in the late 1940s–early 1950s — [EOA ANH](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236).
- Chewaukla Mineral Springs Co. incorporated Feb. 8, 1938 — [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).

## Era: midcentury (1947–1967)

### Narrative
- **Reform and relapse.** March 1947 grand jury investigated McLaughlin; he did not run again; indicted for bribery, illegal voting and misuse of funds but never convicted; died May 5, 1958 — [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/). Earl T. Ricks mayor and I.G. Brown sheriff (1947–49), both decorated WWII aviators — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). McMath governor 1949–1953 — [EOA GI Revolt](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/GI-Revolt-4157). Airport renamed Hot Springs Memorial Field May 1947; Chicago & Southern service 1948 (merged into Delta 1953) — [City: Airport History](https://www.hotspringsar.gov/476/Airport-History). After Faubus's 1954 election casino gambling resumed — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). The Vapors opened 1960; Belvedere bombed 1959; Vapors bombed Jan. 4, 1963; a 1961 federal probe called Hot Springs the largest illegal gambling operation in the U.S.; Madden died April 24, 1965; Rockefeller's State Police raids August 1967 — details in gangster-era-and-gambling.md.
- **Bathing decline.** 1950s decline attributed to new medical treatments and the end of illegal gambling — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/). Free Bathhouse served indigents until 1956 — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm). Fordyce closed June 29, 1962, first on the Row — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row); [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). Grand Promenade completed 1958 — [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm) (conflict: "early 1960s" per [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row)). Noble Fountain moved to the Reserve Ave. end 1957; balustrade bandstand removed 1958; Quapaw display spring covered with plate glass mid-1950s; Ozark cooling tanks removed 1953; Administration Building AC replaced 1960, first floor remodeled 1966; jug fountain in front installed 1966 — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Hospital.** U.S. Army took control 1957; April 1, 1960 passed to the state (sold for $1 per Wikipedia) as the Hot Springs Rehabilitation Center, one of nine in the nation and the only one west of the Mississippi — [EOA Army-Navy Hospital](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Arkansas-Career-Training-Institute-2236); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Industry and lakes.** Alliance Rubber 1954, Norris Dispensers 1957, Lake Catherine Footwear 1960; in the 1950s Blakely Mountain Dam flooded Buckville and Cedar Glades to create Lake Ouachita — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). Blakely Mountain Dam built 1948–1953 by USACE (231 ft tall, 1,100 ft crest, 75 MW) — [Wikipedia: Lake Ouachita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ouachita). Lake Ouachita State Park established 1953, opened 1955 — [Wikipedia: LOSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ouachita_State_Park). DeGray Lake authorized 1950, water supply added 1958, built 1962–1972 — [Wikipedia: DeGray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeGray_Lake).
- **Motor-hotel boom.** 1950–1965 brought a major hotel and motel building boom tied to casino gambling; the later crackdown hurt the business; Velda Rose Tower Motor Hotel on Park Avenue built by Garland Anthony and named for his daughter, dated 1960 or 1963 (conflict) — [Rex Nelson, "Along Park Avenue"](https://www.rexnelsonsouthernfried.com/post/along-park-avenue); [KKYR](https://kkyr.com/arkansas-hotel-makes-list-of-national-register-of-historic-places/); 10 stories, 191 rooms, covered parking deck — [OfficeSpace listing](https://www.officespace.com/ar/hot-springs/1368578-217-park-ave). Park Avenue motor courts (Bellaire, Cottage Courts, Cove, Lynwood, Parkway, Perry Plaza, Taylor Rosamond, Wheatley Courts) plus Butchie's Drive-In, Opal's Steak House and Langdon Filling Station (mostly NRHP Feb. 11, 2004) — [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas).
- **Clinton years.** Family moved from Hope in 1953 after his mother married Roger Clinton Sr., an auto dealer; 1011 Park Avenue (Birnbaum-Shubetz House) about 1954–1961, then 213 Scully Street; St. John's Catholic, Ramble Elementary and Hot Springs High (graduated 1964); baptized at Park Place Baptist, 721 Park Ave.; prom at the Arlington — [EOA Bill Clinton Boyhood Home (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bill-clinton-boyhood-home-7952/); [Arkansas Tourism](https://www.arkansas.com/billgrimage-clinton-sites); [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/). Roger Clinton Jr. born July 25, 1956; Billy Bob Thornton born Aug. 4, 1955 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Integration.** School desegregation began 1956 with a high-school auto-mechanics course; bathhouses desegregated 1965 — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Hot Springs delayed integration for more than a decade after Brown v. Board — [Wikipedia: HSHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_High_School_(Arkansas)) (summary garbled; verify). National Baptist Convention bought the Woodmen building 1948 (NPS; Preserve Arkansas/Wikipedia: 1950) — [NPS AfAm](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- **Baseball.** Bathers (1947–55) titles 1948 and 1950; White Sox trained 1948–51 at Jaycee Field; 1952 Hank Aaron (18) played at Majestic Park (Indianapolis Clowns; a Negro League World Series game); April 15, 1953 Bathers reinstated after the Tugerson expulsion vote; Oct. 22, 1953 Jackie Robinson exhibition at Majestic Park; Cotton States League folded after 1955 — [Wikipedia: Bathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Bathers); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [Trail site](https://hotspringsbaseballtrail.com/see-the-trail/). Bobby Mitchell graduated 1953 (school affiliation **[UNCONFIRMED]**, possibly Langston) — [Wikipedia: HSHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_High_School_(Arkansas)).
- **Attractions.** IQ Zoo 1955 at 380 Whittington Ave., later 422 Central (EoA caption: 1954–1990) — [EoA: Keller Breland (snippet)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/keller-bramwell-breland-2530/); Ostrich Farm closed 1953; Dryden Pottery moved here December 1956 — [EOA (snippet)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/?p=24833); Tiny Town moved to its building 1963 — [Roadside America (snippet)](https://www.roadsideamerica.com/story/11287); Jack Bridges Jr. and Sue bought the gator farm 1965 — [farm site](https://alligatorfarmzoo.com/about-us). Miss Arkansas Pageant in Hot Springs from 1958 — [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Verna Cook Garvan began her garden in 1956 — [Wikipedia: Garvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garvan_Woodland_Gardens).
- **Oaklawn.** Season from 30 days (1940s) to 43 days in 1961; 1962 fifth-highest profit in North America; 1965 Derby purse $50,000 — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/); [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort). Oaklawn remained the only legal gambling after 1967 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Passenger rail ended 1964 — [EOA HS Railroad](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157).

### Daily life
- Nightlife: The Vapors (coffee shop, Monte Carlo Room, theater restaurant with retractable stage, late-night casino; Edgar Bergen, the Smothers Brothers, Tony Bennett, Liberace, Patti Page, Les Paul, Phyllis Diller, Mickey Rooney) — [EOA Vapors](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/vapors-3998/).
- Motor courts and drive-ins along Park Avenue (Butchie's Drive-In, Opal's Steak House) — [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas).
- IQ Zoo chickens walked tightropes, dispensed souvenirs and fortune cards and "danced" to jukebox music — [APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2008/09/animals).
- Brothels: Maxine Temple Jones (1945–early 1970s) arrested 1963, two years in Cummins Prison, pardoned by Rockefeller after giving information on gambling — [EoA: Maxine Temple Jones](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/maxine-temple-jones-5338/).
- Linen postcards (1930s–1950s) and the "Large letter" Greetings layout define the tourist image — [Collectors Weekly](https://collectorsweekly.com/postcards/linen).

Also casino staff, entertainers, motel operators, bath attendants (declining), state rehab center staff (from 1960), animal trainers (IQ Zoo), gator farmers.

## Era: revival (1968–1999)

### Narrative
- **The empty Row.** Maurice closed 1974 ([KUAF 2025](https://www.kuaf.com/show/ozarks-at-large/2025-08-21/maurice-bathhouse-in-hot-springs-to-undergo-32-million-renovation)); Ozark 1977; Hale 1978 (a 1981 theater conversion lasted nine months); Superior 1983 (EOA) or 1984 (NPS) — conflict; Quapaw 1984; Lamar November 1985; by 1985 only the Buckstaff was left — [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm); [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/); [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). Health Services Inc. ran the Quapaw briefly from 1968 with 20 tubs; by 1979 only 96,000 baths were given on the Row — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). The Pythian ran part-time and closed 1974; the National Baptist Hotel closed 1983 (its manager: "It's a small price to pay for integration") — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); conflict: 1981 closure per [Preserve Arkansas (via search summary)](https://preservearkansas.org/woodmen-of-union-building-national-baptist-hotel/).
- **Preservation.** Bathhouse Row on the National Register Nov. 13, 1974 (ref. 74000275); Garland County Courthouse NRHP 1979; Central Avenue HD NRHP June 25, 1985; Bathhouse Row + Grand Promenade National Historic Landmark District 1987 (May 28) — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm); [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/); [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). NPS created the Thermal Water Cascade in 1982 — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/). Fordyce reopened May 13, 1989 as visitor center and museum — same. Ozark skylights rehabilitated 1983; Promenade paving brick replaced 1984 — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Hot Springs Mountain Tower**, 216 ft: construction began 1982 (contract March), opened June 3, 1983 — [Wikipedia: Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Mountain_Tower); [NPS Tower](https://www.nps.gov/places/hot-springs-mountain-tower.htm); conflict: dated 1982 (Wikipedia city) vs 1983 (EOA) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Rix Tower came down July 13, 1971 (NPS) or 1975 (Wikipedia).
- **Hot Springs Village.** John A. Cooper Sr. (Cooper Communities) approached in 1969; incorporated Jan. 21, 1970, broke ground Feb. 15, opened June 1, 1970 on about 20,000 acres bought from Dierks Forests; gated (five gates approved 1995); straddles the Garland–Saline line; 57.64 sq mi — [EOA Hot Springs Village](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-village-garland-and-saline-counties-888/). 11 recreation lakes plus 1 water-supply lake — [Wikipedia: HSV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Village,_Arkansas).
- **Institutions.** 1969 Council for the Liberation of Black (CLOB) and Quapaw Technical Institute; 1973 Garland County Community College — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). St. Gabriel School closed 1968; HSHS moved to its Emory Street campus 1968 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [Wikipedia: HSHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_High_School_(Arkansas)). Mid-America Science Museum opened Jan. 20, 1979 and was dedicated by Gov. Bill Clinton April 22, 1979 (conceived by Gov. Winthrop Rockefeller in the 1960s; authorized 1971) — [Museum history (snippet)](https://midamericamuseum.org/history/). National Park Medical Center 1985; Garvan Woodland Gardens founded 1985; ASMSA 1993 — [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Verna Garvan died 1993, bequeathing the garden — [Wikipedia: Garvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garvan_Woodland_Gardens). 1986 City of Hot Springs v. Creviston (revenue bonds need voter consent) — [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- **Magic Springs** opened July 22, 1978 as "Magic Springs Family Fun Park" (built by Bob Sykes; managed by Leisure and Recreation Concepts under Michael Jenkins); sold in the 1980s to a group led by Melvyn Bell; Arkansas Twister opened May 30, 1992; closed 1995; Fitraco (Belgium) bought it at foreclosure — [Wikipedia: Magic Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Springs); [Coasterpedia](https://coasterpedia.net/wiki/Magic_Springs_and_Crystal_Falls). See magic-springs.md.
- **Oaklawn.** Charles J. Cella president 1968; Racing Festival of the South 1974; five-level glass grandstand enclosure 1975; 1982 record opening crowd 56,214 [KATV (snippet)](https://katv.com/sports/content/15000-attend-opening-day-at-oaklawn); 1983 handle record $168,740,923 and attendance 1,303,223; Sunny's Halo (1983) first Arkansas Derby winner to win the Kentucky Derby [KentuckyDerby.com (snippet)](https://kentuckyderby.com/?p=21445); 62 days by 1984; simulcasting and Sunday racing 1989; 240×14-ft mural 1992; instant racing 1999 — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/); [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort).
- **The Vapors** added the Cockeyed Cowboy and Apollo Disco 1977; Dane Harris died 1981; sold to Tower of Strength Ministries October 1998 — [EOA Vapors](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/vapors-3998/).
- **Ruth Coker Burks** cared for more than 1,000 AIDS patients from the mid-1980s to the mid-1990s and conducted about 43 burials; 1989 Angela Graboys-Rudner became Arkansas's first full-time female rabbi — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- **May 1990 flood.** Nearly 13 inches of rain over a weekend; shops across from the Row took several feet of water; bathhouse basements filled to the ceiling; the newly renovated Fordyce's electrical and HVAC swamped; thermal pumps and reservoirs contaminated; thermal water to fountains and bathhouses shut off — [NPS Morning Report via npshistory.com](https://npshistory.com/morningreport/incidents/hosp.htm). No source found for a notable 1997 flood.
- Maxine's building renovated 1989, reopened 1991 as Maxine's Coffee House and Puzzle Bar — [EoA: Maxine Temple Jones](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/maxine-temple-jones-5338/). Mountain Valley HQ renovation completed March 12, 1988 — [EoA This Day Mar 12, 1988](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/this-day-ar-history/this-day-2730/). Documentary Film Festival first held 1992 (conflict: 1991 per Wikipedia) — [EOA](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-documentary-film-institute-3816/). Hot Springs Music Festival 1995 (EOA) / 1996 (Wikipedia) — [EOA HSMF](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-music-festival-4702/). Clinton boyhood home NRHP May 18, 1995 — [EOA](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bill-clinton-boyhood-home-7952/). Hanamaki, Japan sister city 1993 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **[inferred]** 1967–1988 "empty Row" = "town in decline" sandbox; from 1989 the job becomes adaptive reuse (lease a bathhouse, pick a use, restore glass, survive floods).

### Daily life
- Retirement and lake living: Hot Springs Village; DeGray Lake Resort State Park (established 1974, 94-room lodge, 18-hole golf course of about 7,100 yards) — [Wikipedia: DeGray SP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeGray_Lake_Resort_State_Park). Theme-park summers at Magic Springs (1978–1995). Bath attendants mostly African American until the 1980s — [NPS AfAm](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm). Hot Springs Village Voice founded 1990 — [EOA HSV](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-village-garland-and-saline-counties-888/).
- Friday-night football: Lakeside vs Lake Hamilton played every year from 1968 (except two 1970s years) until paused after 1999 — [THV11 (via search summary)](https://www.thv11.com/article/sports/high-school/football/lake-hamilton-vs-lakeside-rivalry-renewed-after-nearly-two-decades-of-silence-tackling-traditions/91-598027047).

## Era: modern (2000–2026)

### Narrative
- **Magic Springs** reopened May 27, 2000 as "Magic Springs and Crystal Falls" after Hot Springs voters approved a bond issue; PARC Management took over June 10, 2008; EPR Properties (owner) with Premier Parks (operator) from late 2016/2017; "Crystal Falls" name dropped 2017; the Arkansas Twister's official retirement after the 2025 season — [Wikipedia: Magic Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Springs); [Wikipedia: Arkansas Twister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Twister). See magic-springs.md.
- **Adaptive reuse of the Row.** Quapaw: NPS restoration 2004; Quapaw Baths & Spa July 2008. Ozark: Museum of Contemporary Art 2009–2013; HSNP Cultural Center 2014. Superior: brewery 2012 (Wikipedia; some say 2013). Fordyce closed October 2012 for repairs, reopened 2014 (Lamar as interim visitor center). Maurice, Ozark and Hale offered for lease February 2014. Hotel Hale opened May 2019 (nine rooms piped with thermal water; Mayor Pat McCabe and Ellen McCabe). Lamar: park offices + Bathhouse Row Emporium — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row); [NPS Hale case study (via search summary)](http://home.nps.gov/articles/000/hale-batthouse.htm). The Row was on the National Trust's "11 Most Endangered" list in 2003 (removed May 2007) — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). Maurice: vacant since 1974; ground broken August 2025 on a $31.6M (~$32M) Great American Outdoors Act restoration; Supt. Laura Miller plans an eventual lease; still closed April 2026, when a local group was raising money to re-create its original stained-glass ceiling — [KUAF 2025](https://www.kuaf.com/show/ozarks-at-large/2025-08-21/maurice-bathhouse-in-hot-springs-to-undergo-32-million-renovation); [NPS Maurice GAOA groundbreaking (via search summary)](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/news/maurice-gaoa-groundbreaking.htm); [ADG 2026-04-11 (via search summary)](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2026/apr/11/hot-springs-group-raising-money-to-re-create/).
- Josie Fernandez first female superintendent 2004; the Hot Springs quarter (first America the Beautiful quarter) released April 19, 2010 — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- **Fires and floods.** July 2001 fire gutted two downtown buildings including the Ohio Club — [Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2001/jul/12/historic-gambling-spot-gutted-by-fire/). Majestic closed 2006; Feb. 27, 2014 fire destroyed the older yellow-brick section (fought nearly 20 hours, no injuries; burned section razed within days in a ~12-hour operation); city acquired it 2015; board voted June 21, 2016 to demolish; demolished late 2016 — [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/); [EoA: Majestic Hotel](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/majestic-hotel-8351/); [EoA This Day June 21, 2016](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/this-day-ar-history/june-21-2016/); [NWA Online, Mar 1 2014](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2014/mar/01/burned-hotel-building-being-razed-20140301/). 2014: nearly all of downtown on the state's endangered list (May 28); APA named Central Avenue a "Great Street" (Oct. 1) — [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/). May 19–20, 2020 two flood waves covered Central Avenue, flooded the Row and floated cars; about 85 people rescued — [KARK (via search summary)](https://digital-release.kark.com/?p=352188).
- **Oaklawn.** 2004 centennial (Smarty Jones won the $5M bonus); 2005 "games of skill" and the Eclipse Award of Merit; 2008 $3M slots/poker/Instant Racing addition; 2015 American Pharoah; Louis Cella president 2017 (Charles J. Cella d. Dec. 6, 2017); $100M hotel and event center announced 2018, completed 2021; full casino April 1, 2019 (Amendment 100); renamed Oaklawn Racing Casino Resort 2019; only Forbes four-star spa in Arkansas since 2024 — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/); [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort); [Wikipedia: Charles J. Cella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_J._Cella). See oaklawn-and-racing.md.

Gangster Museum of America opened April 2008 — [EoA](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/gangster-museum-of-america-5918/). Vapors listed for sale Nov. 2013, bought 2017 to become "Vapors Live" — [EOA Vapors](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/vapors-3998/). Arlington bought 2017 by Al Rajabi's Sky Capital Group of San Antonio; turned 100 Nov. 28, 2024; $30M renovation begun 2023 and finishing 2025, stripping seven layers of stucco to the brick and redoing the roof and cooling tower — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/); [EOA Arlington (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arlington-hotel-2987/); [Arlington history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/). ## Sensitive history: handling notes

- **Slavery (`frontier`).** 1860 Hot Spring County had 613 enslaved of 5,635; many worked in hotels; one hotel advertised baths for enslaved people, who were then returned to their owners — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Two enslaved men belonging to Dunbar were part of the 1804 party — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/). Notes' guidance: a historically honest game must depict it carefully and cannot leave it out ("handle with educational care").
- **Segregated bathing (`gilded-spa`–`midcentury`).** Restricted hours for Black bathers in the 1880s (not 10 a.m.–noon); Free Bathhouse segregated 1898 and in four sections from 1922; Black-run Crystal (1904), Pythian (1914) and Woodmen of Union (1923–24); Maurice reopened the Independent as whites-only after A.C. Page's year; bathhouses desegregated 1965 — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Frame Black-owned businesses as achievement against Jim Crow rules (notes' Crystal storyline).
- **Lynchings, executions and violence (`gilded-spa`, `roaring`).** Will Norman, an African American, lynched June 19, 1913 before an estimated crowd of 5,000; Gilbert Harris lynched 1922; Harry Poe (1910) and Clarence Schumann (1913) legally executed; Oscar Chitwood murdered at the courthouse Dec. 26, 1910; 1919 nightriding in the "Little Georgia" area led to Housley v. State — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). Notes: "sensitive; include with care." Recommendation: museum-style text only, never gameplay.
- **1913 fire origin.** Began in Ulma Madlock's home (ADG: a Black-owned home laundry); the People's Laundry at 356 Malvern Ave. was dynamited — [EOA Fire 1913](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Hot-Springs-Fire-of-1913-14306); [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/). Avoid framing that blames the household.
- **Travel segregation.** Mitchell v. United States (1941) — [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- **School segregation.** Langston High for Black students from 1913; desegregation began 1956; integration delayed more than a decade after Brown — [Wikipedia: HSHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_High_School_(Arkansas)); [EOA HS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Bobby Mitchell (1953) probably attended Langston **[UNCONFIRMED]**.
- **Tugerson, 1953.** The Bathers signed Jim and Leander Tugerson; league owners voted 6-0 (Pine Bluff abstaining) to expel the club; the national association reinstated it April 15, 1953; per Wikipedia the brothers never appeared in a regular-season game for Hot Springs and began with the Knoxville Smokies — [Wikipedia: Bathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Bathers). Notes: present factually as a museum-style card, not a playable scenario.
- **Native history.** Use "Caddo-ancestral homeland… Quapaw-claimed"; present neutral-ground and de Soto stories only as legend; draw the Quapaw Bathhouse "Indian head" cartouche abstractly, never as caricature (visual_style.md).
- **Disease and charity.** Ral City (syphilis euphemism), mercury rubs, the 1921 VD clinic, polio wards and Ruth Coker Burks's AIDS care — handle respectfully.
- **Real people.** Clinton, Capone, Madden, McLaughlin and Burks cameos may need legal review; living people appear only in factual trivia, never as playable caricatures.
