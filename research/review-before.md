# Valley of Vapors: Through the Ages — Adversarial Art Direction Review (BEFORE)

Reviewer stance: blunt comparison against benchmark A, benchmark B, benchmark C, benchmark D (Steam store shots).

## Coverage

- Contact sheets viewed: desktop-01, 03, 04, 05, 08, 09, 10, 11, 12; mobile-01 through mobile-08. Desktop-02, 06 and 07 did not load (the image request limit dropped them). Their frames (help, inventory, shop, journal, dialogue, era-2 intro, ch1 and ch2 farm) are all covered by the mobile sheets and by the full-size frames below.
- Full-size frames viewed: desktop 01-title, 04-game, 15-ripe, 19-night, 21-rain, 25-ridge, 28-animals, 35-wipe, 40-ch1-farm, 45-ch6-farm, 46-ch1-town, 48-ch3-town, 49-ch4-town, 50-ch5-town, 51-ch6-town, 53-ch4-night, 54-ch6-lake; mobile 04-game, 18-dusk, 48-ch3-town.
- Benchmarks viewed: A-1, 2, 4; B-1, 3, 5; C-1; D-1. The rest of the batch was dropped by the request limit. The style bar is clear from these eight: hand-made pixel or 3D art, dense set dressing, strong value structure, real lighting.

Overall: the game reads as a clean, competent vector prototype or an educational web game. It does not read as a commercial farming sim. The ideas are strong: era-coded HUD, real signage, sourced history. The rendering is roughly two generations behind the genre.

### Notes per category

**1. Art (3).** The world is a flat green field with speckle noise, tiny purple dots and tufts of grass. There are no tile edges, no ground variety and no clutter density. Buildings are flat front elevations: a rectangle plus a triangle roof, no roof plane seen from above, no thickness, no shadow. There is one lollipop deciduous tree with pink dots and one pine stamp, repeated in grids (46-ch1-town, 64-mill). Rocks are grey ellipses. Characters are about 40px tall front-facing paper dolls with no visible secondary detail. The cow in 28-animals is a grey smudge about 30px wide. Crops in 15-ripe are dark specks on dark soil. Compare benchmark A-1: every tile carries texture, wood grain, tile overlap and props. Our farm frames 40–45 are about 70% featureless green.

**2. Lighting (4).** The night pass works in a basic way: in 19-night, warm pools at doors read "1871 oil lamp". But:
- Lights paint *over* facades instead of pooling on the ground. In 53-ch4-night the street lamps blow out the bathhouse fronts into white-blue smears.
- Windows stay dark at night in 19 and 53. This is the single biggest "lights are on" cue, and it is missing.
- Source types are not differentiated. 1931 lamps look cool white; they should be incandescent or early sodium.
- A soft yellow-green halo follows the player at 06:00, in daylight and in rain (04-game, 13, 21-rain, 24-low-energy, mobile 04). It reads as a bug or a selection glow.
- Nothing casts a shadow: no trees, buildings or people (beyond a faint foot ellipse).
- Dusk (18-dusk) is just "darker". There is no warm sky grade.
- Weather is a flat colour wash. In 22-snow the ground stays green-grey with dots, and roofs and trees have no snow. In 23-fog the wash is flat and pale.

**3. Animation and feel (2).** Every still shows the same front-facing idle pose, even in 11-walk. There are no tool-swing frames, no harvest pop and no item arc. The fishing bite (26) is a "!" and a red dot. Toasts stack as black web pills. The era wipe (35-wipe) is a hard vertical split over a primitive vector landscape. There are no particles visible anywhere except rain streaks and snow dots, and no steam in a game called *Valley of Vapors*.

**4. UI (5).** The HUD is consistent and functional, and the chrome changes per era: dark green for ch1, parchment for ch2 and ch3, navy and gold for ch4, cream and teal for ch5, navy for ch6. That is a genuinely good idea. But:
- The panels look like a web form: rounded rectangles, pill tabs, outline buttons, a "Close Esc" chip.
- Tool icons are thin single-stroke sticks; hoe, axe, pick and scythe are near-identical at a glance.
- Inventory and chest grids are empty grey squares.
- The dialogue box (31-dialogue) has a small upscaled sprite as its portrait. benchmark B-3 and benchmark B-5 use a full-bust portrait and a bold name plate.

**5. Typography (5).** Era-appropriate faces are a strong concept: a serif for 1807–1886, a sans for 1931–2026. But:
- Desktop HUD text is about 11–12px, and the goals panel is tiny (04-game, 48-ch3-town).
- Ch1 grey-on-dark-green has low contrast.
- History cards and the almanac print raw URLs in-game (52-almanac, 59-reduced, 62-quarry-sign, 67-fund).
- The title logo (01-title) sits in a translucent grey box with a system-looking serif. It reads as placeholder.
- There is no display or bitmap face for in-world signage, so every sign uses the same bold serif.

**6. Readability (5).**
- Works: name tags ("Hattie", "Amos") and "!" quest markers are clear, and so are the tile cursor, the hotbar numbers and the paths.
- Fails: crops (15-ripe; the ripe "ring badge" is a faint white circle), animals (28-animals) and tool icons.
- Fails: the east-edge tufa strip reads as concrete sidewalk (46–51); the quarry pits (25-ridge) read as tyres or manholes.
- Fails: the player is too small for the viewport on desktop.

**7. Mobile (6).** It is better than desktop because the camera is effectively zoomed in (mobile 04-game, 48-ch3-town). The art benefits from the larger scale; this is evidence the desktop camera is too far out. Controls are large and clear, and modals fit.
- The HUD (date, money and three buttons, then the goals bar) takes the top 18%. Joystick, A/B and hotbar take the bottom 25%, which leaves about 55% for the world.
- The joystick ring sits on top of world content (mobile 18-dusk covers a building).
- In mobile 48-ch3-town, the date and money pills crowd into one row.

**8. Authenticity (5).**
- The research layer is excellent. Concepts are well chosen: 1807 trade tents and a trapper cabin; 1871 plank "BATHS / BOARDING / STAGE / DRUGS"; 1886 brick hotel and FREE BATHS; 1931 neon CLUB (Southern Club), twin-tower hotel (Arlington), tourist court and radio tower; 1955 DRIVE-IN, MOTOR HOTEL and FARM SUPPLY; 2026 BREWERY in a bathhouse (Superior), SPA, CULTURAL CTR and FARMERS MARKET.
- The rendering undercuts it. Bathhouse Row appears as a 2x2 block of identical boxes on both sides of a street. The real Row is one line of individually designed bathhouses on the east side of Central Avenue, backed by Hot Springs Mountain, the Grand Promenade and magnolias.
- 49-ch4-town and 50-ch5-town have *identical* bathhouse facades, including the same blue oval "BATHS" blimp sign. Only the road paint differs.
- The farm in ch2–ch6 (41–45) is the same green field; only the house and the HUD colour change.
- No frame shows steam, tufa, travertine or hot-water cascades: the defining visual of the valley.

## Ranked gap list (most impactful first)

1. **Empty, untextured ground plane (the farm is a green void).**
   *Evidence:* 40–45 ch1–ch6 farm, 04-game, 21-rain, 24-low-energy, mobile 40–45.
   *Fix:* Build a real autotiled terrain set: 3–4 grass variants with light and dark patch noise at two scales, clover and flower clumps, and dirt-to-grass edge transitions. Paint paths with worn edges instead of drawing rectangles. Seed the farm with benchmark A-style debris (stumps, logs, weeds, saplings, boulders) per era. Target fewer than 30% of tiles being "plain grass" in any frame.

2. **Desktop camera too far out; sprites too small.**
   *Evidence:* desktop 04-game vs mobile 04-game (the mobile crop looks far better); the player is about 1/20 of screen height.
   *Fix:* Zoom desktop 1.75–2x so the player is about 1/10–1/12 of viewport height, which matches the benchmarks. Use integer scaling with nearest-neighbour if you move to pixel art.

3. **No depth: flat elevations, no roofs seen from above, no shadows.**
   *Evidence:* every building in 46–51, 19-night, 64-mill; trees everywhere.
   *Fix:* Redraw buildings in 3/4 top-down: show the roof plane (shingle, tin or tile texture), eaves overhang and a visible wall height. Add a consistent light direction (sun from top-left) with soft cast shadows (a 25–35% multiply polygon) for buildings, trees, fences, rocks and characters. Add AO darkening where walls meet ground.

4. **Water: ruler-straight creek, staircase lake shoreline.**
   *Evidence:* 54-ch6-lake (blocky stepped edge with two flat blues), 04-game and 25-ridge (creek is a straight band with tick marks), 48–51 (left edge strip).
   *Fix:* Marching-squares or 47-tile autotile shorelines with a mud or sand lip, a foam or highlight line and a 3-step depth gradient. Meander the creek and add rocks, reeds and riffles. Add animated sparkle and ripple, and reflections of trees and buildings at low alpha. Hot inflows should steam and tint the water teal-milky.

5. **Eras are not visually distinct in the farm view; at least one chapter has no palette of its own.**
   *Evidence:* 41-ch2-farm vs 42-ch3-farm: same parchment HUD, same grass, same path, only the house differs. 43/44/45 are the same saturated green field.
   *Fix:* Give each era a world colour grade (LUT) plus set dressing, not just HUD chrome. Suggested direction:
   - 1807: muted olive and sepia, canebrake and wild grass.
   - 1871: dusty ochre, raw-plank, split-rail fences.
   - 1886: warmer, brick-red accents, picket fences, cleared fields.
   - 1931: desaturated sepia-cool Depression grade, barbed wire, Model A truck.
   - 1955: Kodachrome saturated warm, red barn, tractor, mailbox.
   - 2026: clean neutral, solar panel, raised beds, EV or e-bike, drip line.
   Give 1871 and 1886 different HUD stock: 1871 rough newsprint, 1886 engraved cream with ornament.

6. **Town layout repeats every era; ch4 and ch5 are near-duplicates.**
   *Evidence:* 47–51 (same 2x2 block grid), 49-ch4-town vs 50-ch5-town (identical bathhouses and blue oval sign).
   *Fix:* Lay out Bathhouse Row as it is: one line of distinct bathhouses on one side of Central Avenue, with the mountain and promenade behind, and the commercial strip and Arlington opposite. Give each bathhouse its own silhouette: a Spanish Revival red-tile roof, the Quapaw's tiled dome as a real dome rather than a blue blimp sign, Neoclassical columns, Mission parapets. Age the Row across eras:
   - 1955: neon and cars.
   - 1985-era closures, if referenced: boarded windows.
   - 2026: restored, with scaffolding on one bathhouse and the brewery patio.

7. **Night lighting overpaints buildings; windows stay dark; light types are undifferentiated; the player has a daytime halo.**
   *Evidence:* 53-ch4-night (white-blue blobs over facades), 19-night (dark windows), 04-game, 13, 21-rain, 24 (glow around the player at 06:00 in daylight and rain).
   *Fix:* Use a separate light-map layer composited *under* building tops and over the ground (ground pools plus wall wash at low intensity only). Make windows emissive after dusk. Set per-era source temperatures:
   - Campfire: 1800K with flicker and embers.
   - Oil lantern: 2000K, small radius.
   - Gas: 2700K, a slightly green-white mantle.
   - Neon: saturated red and blue tube colour plus a coloured halo on the wall.
   - Sodium: about 1900K monochrome orange that desaturates what it hits.
   - LED: 4000–5000K crisp, with hard falloff.
   Limit the player light to after dusk, as a lantern or flashlight carried per era.

8. **Time of day and weather are flat overlays.**
   *Evidence:* 18-dusk (just darker), 22-snow (no ground or roof snow), 23-fog (uniform wash), 21-rain (streaks plus a dark tint only).
   *Fix:* Grade per hour: golden hour warm with long shadows, blue hour cool, night desaturated blue with warm sources. For snow, swap in snow ground tiles, roof and tree caps and footprints. For rain, darken and add specular to wet ground, puddles, splash particles and drip on roofs. For fog, use 2–3 parallax noise layers drifting, densest in the creek bottom.

9. **Characters are tiny, stiff and under-costumed.**
   *Evidence:* 11-walk (same pose as idle), 48-ch3-town, 50-ch5-town, 31-dialogue portrait.
   *Fix:* 4-direction walk cycles of 4–6 frames, tool-swing frames, larger sprites, a drop shadow and silhouettes driven by era costume:
   - 1807: hunting frock and moccasins.
   - 1871: slouch hat and vest.
   - 1886: bustle dress, bowler, bath attendant whites.
   - 1931: fedora, cloche, overalls.
   - 1955: rolled jeans, skirts, ball cap.
   - 2026: fleece, hoodie, sneakers.
   Dialogue needs real bust portraits with 2–3 expressions.

10. **Crops, animals and interactables are illegible.**
    *Evidence:* 15-ripe (dark sprites on dark soil, faint ripe ring), 28-animals (cow is a grey blob in an empty pen), the SHIP bin in 04-game.
    *Fix:* Bigger crop sprites with distinct silhouettes per stage. Ripe should be bright and saturated with a small idle sway and a sparkle. Tilled soil should be warm mid-brown, with a clearly darker wet state. Animals need about 48px bodies, recognisable breeds and idle animations. Add hay, a trough and a water tank to the pen. Make the ship bin a chunky prop with a lid.

11. **The tufa slope on the east edge reads as pavement.**
    *Evidence:* the right-hand grey stippled strip in 46-ch1-town, 48-ch3-town, 49, 50, 51; 04-game top-right.
    *Fix:* Render it as Hot Springs Mountain's tufa and travertine: cream-to-ochre layered terraces with orange and green mineral (algae) streaks, small steaming pools and runnels, ferns and moss at the edges, and an irregular organic boundary. Put it on an elevation with cliff tiles so it reads as hillside, not slab.

12. **Ridge and quarry read as abstract shapes.**
    *Evidence:* 25-ridge (grey sawtooth "mountains" at the top edge, black ring pits like tyres, white crystal clusters), 62-quarry-sign.
    *Fix:* Build the ridge as raised terrain with cliff faces and a forest of oak, hickory and shortleaf pine. Show novaculite as whitish-grey chert outcrops and flake scatter. Show the quarry pits as shallow, leaf-filled depressions with spoil mounds, not black circles.

13. **Vegetation monotony.**
    *Evidence:* 46-ch1-town (two dozen identical lollipop trees), 64-mill, 25-ridge.
    *Fix:* Use 5–6 species with clear silhouettes: shortleaf pine, white oak, dogwood, redbud, sycamore along the creek, sweetgum. Vary scale and shading, cluster them naturally, add understorey shrubs and ferns, and use seasonal palettes. Fall in 21-rain is a good start.

14. **No steam or vapour anywhere in gameplay.**
    *Evidence:* every in-world frame; only 01-title and 35-wipe have steam puffs.
    *Fix:* Particle steam from springs, the tufa runnels, bathhouse vents and the creek at cool hours. Make it denser in winter, at dawn and in the 1807 chapter. This is the brand; it should be in nearly every frame.

15. **The UI reads as a website, not a game.**
    *Evidence:* 05-inventory, 06-craft, 07-shop, 60-rebind, 68-chest, 52-almanac.
    *Fix:* 9-slice framed panels with era ornament (frontier hide or wood, Victorian engraved border, Deco chrome, mid-century boomerang, 2026 clean card). Use chunkier filled tool icons with distinct silhouettes and colour-coded heads. Item slots should have inset bevels. Toasts should be themed to match.

16. **Typography detail.**
    *Evidence:* the 04-game goals panel (about 11px), 52-almanac and 59-reduced (raw URLs), 01-title logo.
    *Fix:* Desktop HUD body text at 14px minimum. Use per-era display faces for signage and titles: Clarendon or wood type for 1871–1886, Deco geometric for 1931, script or atomic for 1955, grotesk for 2026. Show sources as a short cite with the link behind it. Design a real wordmark with steam integrated.

17. **The title screen and era wipe are placeholder.**
    *Evidence:* 01-title, 35-wipe, 69-chapters.
    *Fix:* A painted parallax vista of the valley with layered ridges, steam and a town that changes per unlocked era. Make the wipe a wash or dissolve between the two eras' vistas, with the year stamp in each era's type.

18. **Feedback and "juice" missing.**
    *Evidence:* 16-harvested (no item pop), 26-bite, 27-catch (fish is a dot), 28-animals (text toasts only).
    *Fix:* Item pop arc into the hotbar, hoe dirt puffs, water splash, a squash-and-stretch on harvest, a heart puff on petting, screen-space floating "+$" numbers. Use reduced-motion variants; the reduced-motion setting already exists.

## What is already good (preserve)

- **Era-coded HUD chrome:** palette and typeface shift per chapter. Keep the system; extend it to the world grade and give 1871 and 1886 their own looks.
- **Era-true signage and building programme:** the neon CLUB, DRIVE-IN arrow, MOTOR HOTEL with vertical sign, FARMERS MARKET bunting, BREWERY/SPA/CULTURAL CTR reuse in 2026, and FREE BATHS in 1886. The concepts are right; only the rendering needs lifting.
- **The 1871 night door pools (19-night):** warm and atmospheric. This is the right mood; fix only the dark windows.
- **Fall foliage (21-rain):** the orange trees are the best seasonal read in the build.
- **The history and source layer:** cards, almanac, the legend tag and the heritage-site framing for the quarry (but restyle the URLs).
- **Mobile layout:** the zoomed camera, large joystick and A/B, a hotbar that fits, and modals that fit the viewport. The mobile scale is the target for desktop.
- **Clean, consistent outline style and colour discipline:** nothing is garish, and it is a coherent base to texture up rather than throw away.
- **Accessibility options:** text size 150%, reduced motion, high-contrast HUD and rebindable keys.
- **Interaction affordances:** name tags, "!" quest markers, the tile cursor and contextual prompts ("Space: Harvest washita radish").
