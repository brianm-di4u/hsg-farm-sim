# Critic B (Codex, GPT-6.1 Sol, effort high): loop 0

The painted baseline scores **33/80** and needs substantial work before it meets the pixel-art target. Detailed buildings provide some period character, but smooth textures, softly shaded figures, flat geometric infrastructure and browser-style panels form incompatible visual systems. The strongest assets are architectural material detail and usable phone controls; the largest weaknesses are pixel craft, character fidelity and tool-action feedback. These are diagnostic baseline scores under the brief's explicit painted-tier exception: pixel integrity is inapplicable, test results are not supplied, and `research/pixel-budget.md` is missing, so no hard-gate pass is established. Capture coverage also limits the review: R11 shows no dialogue, R15's pen is empty, and R16 shows no neighbours.

1. **`pixel` — 2/10.** `crops/R04-row` exposes softly resampled shingles, foliage and lettering rather than deliberate pixel clusters. `crops/R02-player` combines continuous character shading with finely textured painted timber; `desktop/R07` adds flat geometric dock planks over softly textured water. There is no coherent art-pixel size, outline treatment or palette discipline across these asset families. **Comparison:** `benchmarks/stardew-1.jpg` establishes materials with discrete ramps and strong silhouettes; `benchmarks/chained-5.jpg` integrates ground, awnings, people and props through consistent clusters. This baseline falls below the rubric's 3-point anchor because an intentional pixel-art system is not yet visible.

2. **`characters` — 3.5/10.** Faces, hands and limbs are discernible, and hat changes provide some identification. Nevertheless, the heirs share essentially the same rounded body, face and posture, with clothing largely expressed through recolouring. 3. **`environment` — 5/10.** `desktop/R04` has convincing distinctions between timber, brick, roof shingles and decorative trim. `desktop/R03` also establishes recognisable crop species and growth stages. However, `desktop/R02` and `desktop/R17` devote large areas to repeating grass with isolated props. `desktop/R15` exposes fence runs made from disconnected, ladder-like segments, while its wagon appears suspended above a detached oval shadow. `desktop/R07` juxtaposes a minimally shaded dock with dense water texture and large translucent polygons. **Comparison:** `benchmarks/stardew-1.jpg` connects buildings, planting, storage and animals into a purposeful farm; `benchmarks/chained-5.jpg` uses merchandise, rugs, containers and shadows to make a town feel inhabited. The baseline has detailed individual illustrations but weak connective dressing and inconsistent construction.

4. **`lighting` — 4.5/10.** `desktop/R05` deserves credit for warm street lamps, illuminated entrances and a differently coloured club sign. Its night is recognisably different from `desktop/R06` at dusk. But light remains dominated by smooth pools and broad darkness, without clearly resolved directional shadows or convincing light response on characters. `desktop/R08` washes almost the entire gameplay layer in pale fog; `crops/R08-player` compounds this with a lamp and roof obscuring the player. `desktop/R10` conveys winter through ground replacement, flakes and pale overlays, but snow does not consistently describe roof accumulation. **Comparison:** `benchmarks/seastars-2.jpg` preserves distinct silhouettes, crisp surface detail and grounded shadows within coloured night lighting; `benchmarks/chained-5.jpg` grounds daylight objects through consistent shadows. Mood exists here, but neither grid discipline nor lighting structure reaches those anchors.

5. **`animation` — 3.5/10.** `strips/R13` and `strips/R13-mobile` show genuine arm and leg changes, including extended strides; this is more than a static sprite sliding. Several adjacent samples repeat similar poses, however, and the strips demonstrate little secondary motion. More seriously, `strips/R14` shows a raised hoe in frame 1 followed by seven near-identical idle poses, with no visible ground strike, soil change or impact effect. This capture does not establish a complete tool action. **Comparison:** the rubric's SNES/Harvest Moon baseline calls for a basic directional walk and short tool swing; its Stardew-level anchor requires anticipation, impact and recovery. `benchmarks/snes-harvestmoon-1.png` supplies the visual reference, but, as a still, provides no independent evidence of animation quality. The score rests on the supplied motion strips, not inferred benchmark motion.

6. **`ui` — 3.5/10.** `desktop/R12` and `mobile/R12` have understandable inventory hierarchy, visible selection and a useful item-description area. `desktop/R18` presents prices and purchase actions clearly. Their rounded panels, smooth borders, anti-aliased typography and detailed painted icons nevertheless read as web interfaces. The bright book/backpack symbols in `mobile/R04` introduce another icon style. `desktop/R01` and `mobile/R01` reinforce the illustrated rather than pixel-art presentation. **Comparison:** `benchmarks/stardew-5.jpg` unifies portrait, lettering and wooden frame; `benchmarks/seastars-4.jpg` keeps typography, equipment icons and selection treatment coherent. R11 supplies no dialogue box to compare against the dialogue anchor, and R12 does not expose the crafting-tab contents.

7. **`readability` — 6/10.** `mobile/R04` keeps the player, building labels, money and current objective legible at the specified phone viewport. `mobile/R12` successfully reflows the inventory, and the large phone action buttons are easy to distinguish. The remaining problems concern gameplay state: `desktop/R03` and `crops/R03-player` allow mature crops to cover the player's torso and legs; watering drops and harvest sparkles compete with plant forms. In `mobile/R05`, the target square has weak contrast against the dark street. `desktop/R08` nearly erases useful value separation through fog and foreground occlusion. **Comparison:** `benchmarks/stardew-1.jpg` separates crops and actors more decisively through outlines and value; `benchmarks/seastars-2.jpg` retains clearer character definition despite its night palette. Phone UI is serviceable, but world-state readability is inconsistent. No phone crop-stage capture is supplied.

8. **`authenticity` — 5/10.** The eras change more than palette: `desktop/R16` has canvas trading structures, `desktop/R09` wooden buildings and a mud street, `desktop/R04` ornate Victorian architecture, `desktop/R05` five-globe lamps and a club, and `desktop/R06` a drive-in, motor hotel and marked asphalt. `desktop/R17` adds a bungalow and early truck. Those are useful chronological signals. Fidelity remains uneven: Ada and Nora fail the supplied clothing silhouettes; R09 repeats two bathhouse designs; and R04's generic ornate buildings do not establish the research's distinctive white frame hotel or ironclad bathhouse. R07's dock and water provide little evidence specific to 2026 or Lake Hamilton. **Comparison:** `benchmarks/chained-5.jpg` and `benchmarks/stardew-1.jpg` demonstrate stronger internal setting coherence, not historical accuracy. Historical deductions here come from the supplied `research/visual-eras.md` and character checklist. The visible era signatures earn credit, but insufficient reference-specific detail keeps this below the 6-point gate.
